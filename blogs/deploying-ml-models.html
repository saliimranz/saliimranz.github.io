Deploying ML Models Without the Pain: My Production Playbook

March 10, 2026 4 min read By Ali Imran

From model registry to canary rollouts — the infrastructure patterns I rely on for shipping ML to production with confidence. Covers MLflow for experiment tracking, Docker-based serving with FastAPI, automated drift detection, shadow deployments, and the monitoring stack that catches issues before users do.

Track everything in a model registry

Every model that hits production must be reproducible. MLflow (or W&B Models, or a registry of your choice) gives you a versioned record of: training data hash, hyperparameters, evaluation metrics, and the artifact itself. When something breaks in production, the registry is what lets you roll back to a known-good version in minutes instead of days.

Serve with FastAPI in a Docker container

FastAPI strikes the right balance: fast enough for most workloads, idiomatic Python, automatic OpenAPI docs. Pair it with a lean Docker image and a health-check endpoint that loads the model on startup and reports ready/not-ready cleanly. The boring choice is the right choice here.

Shadow before canary, canary before full

New model? Don't ship it to 100% of traffic. Shadow deploy first — the new model runs on real production traffic but its predictions don't reach users. Compare predictions offline. Then canary — 5% of traffic. Watch metrics. Then ramp.

This pattern catches integration bugs, latency regressions, and prediction drift that benchmarks miss.

Drift detection is non-optional

Models silently degrade as the real-world distribution shifts. Track input feature distributions (KS test, PSI) and prediction distributions over time. Alert when drift exceeds threshold. Most teams discover drift weeks late — instrument it from day one.

The monitoring trio

Prometheus for metrics (latency, throughput, error rate, prediction distributions). Grafana for dashboards. Sentry or similar for errors. Wire alerts to PagerDuty. The stack is opinionated but every piece earns its place when the on-call page goes off at 3am.
